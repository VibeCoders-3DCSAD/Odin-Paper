# Chapter 1

## INTRODUCTION

The personal financial management (PFM) industry encompasses tools, applications, and systems designed to help individuals track expenses, manage budgets, monitor savings, and oversee debt obligations. In the Philippines, the demand for such tools has grown as more Filipinos seek to improve their financial well-being amid rising living costs and evolving economic conditions. Financial management applications provide users with mechanisms to record transactions, categorize spending, set financial goals, and gain visibility into their overall financial status. These tools serve individuals across various income levels and employment arrangements, offering varying degrees of automation and analytical capability.

Despite the availability of PFM applications, many Filipinos continue to face persistent challenges in improving their savings and managing debt. A significant portion of the population struggles with maintaining consistent savings due to irregular income, competing financial priorities, and limited access to personalized financial guidance. Debt management remains a concern, particularly for individuals who rely on multiple credit sources or informal lending arrangements. Traditional PFM applications often emphasize generic expense tracking without adequately addressing the specific needs of users seeking to improve savings outcomes or reduce debt burdens. Additionally, many existing applications are not localized to Filipino financial realities, including common practices such as family support contributions, informal group savings arrangements, and government-mandated contributions.

Information systems and data-driven technologies offer potential solutions to these financial management challenges. Intelligent systems can analyze user financial profiles, identify spending patterns, forecast future expenses, and provide personalized recommendations for budgeting and debt management. Machine learning models can classify users according to their financial characteristics, detect anomalous transactions that may indicate overspending or fraud, and generate forecasts that support proactive financial planning. These capabilities, when integrated into accessible mobile applications, can provide users with decision-support information that traditional manual tracking methods cannot efficiently deliver.

For Filipino users aged 18 to 59 in the National Capital Region, the specific challenge lies in the absence of PFM tools that prioritize savings and debt outcomes while accounting for local financial behaviors and constraints. Existing applications frequently focus on general money management rather than targeted improvements in savings rates or debt reduction. Furthermore, few applications integrate profile classification, expense forecasting, budget optimization, and anomalous transaction detection into a unified system designed specifically to address savings and debt challenges. This gap limits the ability of users to receive personalized, data-driven guidance that reflects their actual financial circumstances and behavioral patterns.

These conditions establish the need for a localized, intelligent PFM application that addresses the specific savings and debt management requirements of Filipino users. By integrating multiple analytical models within a single platform, such a system could provide users with meaningful insights, actionable recommendations, and decision-support information tailored to their financial profiles. This study proposes the development and evaluation of such a system to determine its potential in improving savings and debt indicators among the target population.

## PROJECT CONTEXT

The beneficiaries of this study are Filipinos aged 18 to 59 who are living or working in the National Capital Region (NCR) and who are interested in improving their savings and debt allocation and management. Based on a preliminary investigation conducted through a pre-survey involving 47 respondents primarily from Taguig, Pasay, Manila, and Makati, the study identified common financial management practices among the target users. Currently, many individuals record their financial transactions through manual methods such as notebooks, spreadsheets, or basic mobile applications that lack intelligent analytical capabilities. Income sources, recurring expenses, debt obligations, and savings allocations are often tracked separately or not tracked at all, resulting in limited visibility into overall financial status.

The preliminary assessment revealed several specific difficulties encountered by the target users. Many respondents reported difficulty in maintaining consistent savings due to insufficient understanding of their spending patterns and cash flow. The absence of personalized insights made it challenging to identify areas where expenses could be reduced or reallocated toward savings or debt repayment. Users also expressed difficulty in anticipating future expenses, leading to inadequate preparation for recurring financial obligations. Additionally, the lack of localized tools that account for Filipino financial practices such as family support, informal savings arrangements, and government contributions further limited the relevance of existing applications to their actual circumstances. These findings, gathered through the pre-survey, form the basis for the identified problems addressed by this study.

The effects of these difficulties manifest in poor savings outcomes and challenges in debt management. Without clear visibility into their financial behavior, users may continue spending patterns that prevent them from achieving savings goals or making progress on debt reduction. The absence of expense forecasting capability results in reactive rather than proactive financial decision-making, leaving users unprepared for predictable expenses. Furthermore, the lack of personalized recommendations means that users cannot easily determine how to adjust their budgets or allocate resources more effectively. These consequences contribute to financial stress and limit the ability of users to improve their overall financial well-being.

To address these concerns, this study proposes the development of BUDI, an intelligent personal financial management application designed to improve savings and debt outcomes for Filipinos aged 18 to 59 in the National Capital Region. BUDI will incorporate features for cash flow management, recurring expense tracking, income source management, savings goal management, budget management, debt management, and financial reporting. The application will provide users with centralized visibility into their financial activities while offering personalized insights derived from their financial profiles and transaction history. BUDI will be developed as an Android application with offline-capable core features and server-side intelligent modules.

The system will employ a multi-model architecture consisting of four intelligent modules. The Personal Financial Profile (PFP) Classification module, using Support Vector Machine (SVM), will categorize users according to their financial characteristics to enable personalized recommendations. The Expense Forecasting module, using Seasonal Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (SARIMA), will analyze historical transaction data to predict future expenses across different categories and time periods. The Budget Optimization module, using Linear Programming (LP), will generate budget allocations based on user constraints and preferences. The Anomalous Expense Detection module, using Interquartile Range (IQR), will identify transactions that deviate significantly from the user's established baseline. Together, these models will provide decision-support information that assists users in making informed financial decisions without replacing their autonomy.

## PURPOSE AND DESCRIPTION OF THE STUDY

The purpose of this study is to develop and evaluate BUDI, an intelligent personal financial management application intended to improve savings and debt management among Filipinos aged 18 to 59 in the National Capital Region. The study aims to provide a localized, savings-debt-first PFM tool that addresses the limitations of existing applications by integrating profile classification, expense forecasting, budget optimization, and anomalous transaction detection. Through this integration, the study seeks to demonstrate how intelligent systems can support users in achieving better savings outcomes, managing debt more effectively, and developing healthier financial behaviors.

BUDI will provide functions for recording and categorizing financial transactions, managing income sources, tracking recurring expenses, monitoring billers and remittances, setting and tracking savings goals, creating and managing budgets, and overseeing debt obligations. The application will include a dashboard that presents an overview of the user's financial status, along with financial reports that summarize income, expenses, savings progress, and debt status. Authorized users will be able to access their financial information, receive intelligent recommendations, and generate reports for personal monitoring. The system will support offline transaction entry and synchronize data when connectivity is available.

The system will utilize a multi-model architecture where each intelligent module performs a specific analytical function. The PFP Classification module will process user profile and behavioral data to assign users to relevant financial categories. The Expense Forecasting module will use historical transaction data as input to the SARIMA model to generate forecasts of expected expenses for succeeding periods. The Budget Optimization module will apply Linear Programming to generate budget allocations that satisfy user-defined constraints while optimizing for specified objectives. The Anomalous Expense Detection module will use the IQR method to identify transactions that fall outside the user's normal spending patterns. All model outputs will be presented as decision-support information accompanied by explanations, and will be applied only with user approval.

Through the integration of these features and technologies, BUDI is expected to improve financial visibility and provide users with more reliable information for decision-making. The system may assist users in identifying opportunities to increase savings, reduce unnecessary expenses, manage debt more effectively, and develop sustainable financial habits. By localizing the application to Filipino financial realities and prioritizing savings and debt outcomes, BUDI aims to address gaps in existing PFM tools and contribute to improved financial well-being among the target population.

## SCOPE AND LIMITATIONS

The study will be conducted in the National Capital Region (NCR), Philippines, with the target users being Filipinos aged 18 to 59 who are living or working in the region. The research locale was selected because NCR represents a diverse urban population with varying income levels, employment arrangements, and financial management needs. The beneficiaries of the study are the target users themselves, who are intended to benefit from access to an intelligent PFM application designed to address their savings and debt management challenges. The intended users of BUDI are individual Filipino users within the specified age range and location who are interested in improving their savings and debt allocation and management.

The system will cover the following functional areas: user authentication, user profiling, user onboarding, cash flow management, recurring expense management, billers and remittances, income source management, savings goal management, budget management, debt management, user settings, financial reports, privacy controls, consent management, data export, account deletion, offline transaction entry, and synchronization. BUDI will be developed as an Android application using Expo SDK 55, React Native 0.83, and related technologies. Core features will be offline-capable with local storage using SQLite, while intelligent modules will operate server-side with local caching. The system will support expense categories that reflect Filipino financial realities, including family support, government contributions, paluwagan, and ambag.

The intelligent modules of BUDI will employ the following algorithms: Support Vector Machine (SVM) for Personal Financial Profile Classification, Linear Programming (LP) for Budget Optimization, Seasonal Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (SARIMA) for Expense Forecasting, and Interquartile Range (IQR) for Anomalous Expense Detection. The models will be trained and evaluated using data from the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) Family Income and Expenditure Survey (FIES), PSA Household Financial Characteristics and Income (HFCI) dataset, Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Consumer Finance and Inclusion Survey (CFIS), BSP Consumer Finance Survey (CFS), BSP Consumer Expectations Survey (CES), the PUEPS pre-survey, and synthetic Filipino financial personas. Forecasts will be generated for periods and categories with sufficient historical data, and anomaly detection will reflect the user's recorded baseline.

The study acknowledges several limitations. The PUEPS pre-survey used purposive sampling of 47 respondents in NCR, primarily from Taguig, Pasay, Manila, and Makati, which may not fully represent the broader target population. Public Philippine datasets are household-level or aggregate, and synthetic personas and transactions are generated for model training and evaluation, with synthetic-to-real generalization currently in progress. The application relies on manual data entry only, with no bank or e-wallet API integration, automatic transaction import, receipt scanning, or OCR. New users will have no forecast or anomaly-detection baseline until sufficient transaction history accrues. The system is Android-only, with iOS, multi-user, household, workspace, and admin functionality out of scope. BUDI provides decision support and does not constitute licensed financial, investment, tax, or legal advice. Savings and debt features remain subject to further validation from PUEPS findings and subject matter expert consultation.

## OPERATIONAL DEFINITION OF TERMS

**Anomalous Expense Detection.** In this study, it refers to the BUDI module that uses the Interquartile Range (IQR) method to identify transactions that deviate significantly from the user's established spending baseline.

**ARIMA (Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average).** Refers to the time-series forecasting model used by BUDI to analyze historical expense data and generate forecasts of future expenses.

**BUDI (Bawas Utang, Dagdag Ipon).** In this study, it refers to the intelligent personal financial management application developed to improve savings and debt management among Filipinos aged 18 to 59 in the National Capital Region.

**Budget Optimization.** Refers to the BUDI module that uses Linear Programming (LP) to generate budget allocations based on user-defined constraints and preferences.

**Cold Start.** Refers to the period when a new BUDI user has insufficient transaction history for expense forecasting or anomaly detection, during which these features surface as pending.

**Decision Support.** Refers to model outputs that inform user decisions without replacing them, accompanied by explanations and applied only with user approval.

**Descriptive-Developmental.** Refers to the research design used in this study, which focuses on describing and developing a system without hypothesis testing.

**Expense Forecasting.** Refers to the BUDI module that uses the SARIMA model to predict future expenses based on historical transaction data.

**HFCI (Household Financial Characteristics and Income).** Refers to the PSA dataset used in this study for household financial characteristics.

**Intelligent Modules.** Refers to the four required BUDI models: Personal Financial Profile Classification, Budget Optimization, Expense Forecasting, and Anomalous Expense Detection.

**IQR (Interquartile Range).** Refers to the statistical method used by the Anomalous Expense Detection module to identify transactions that fall outside the user's normal spending patterns.

**LP (Linear Programming).** Refers to the optimization technique used by the Budget Optimization module to generate budget allocations.

**Paluwagan.** Refers to an informal group savings arrangement common in the Philippines, included as an expense category in BUDI to reflect Filipino financial realities.

**Personal Financial Management (PFM).** Refers to the category of applications and systems designed to help individuals track expenses, manage budgets, monitor savings, and oversee debt obligations.

**Personal Financial Profile (PFP) Classification.** Refers to the BUDI module that uses Support Vector Machine (SVM) to categorize users according to their financial characteristics.

**PUEPS.** Refers to the pre-survey used as preliminary investigation for this study, involving 47 respondents in the National Capital Region.

**SARIMA (Seasonal Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average).** Refers to the time-series forecasting model used by the Expense Forecasting module to predict future expenses.

**Savings-Debt-First PFM.** Refers to a PFM approach that prioritizes improving savings and debt outcomes over generic expense tracking.

**SVM (Support Vector Machine).** Refers to the machine learning algorithm used by the Personal Financial Profile Classification module to categorize users.

**Synthetic Filipino Financial Personas.** Refers to artificially generated user profiles and transaction data used for model training, validation, and testing in the absence of granular behavioral transaction data from public Philippine datasets.
