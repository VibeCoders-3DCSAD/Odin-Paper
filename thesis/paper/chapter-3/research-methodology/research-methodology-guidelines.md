# 3.2 Research Methodology

Research Methodology explains how the research procedures will be carried out. It answers: Where will the study be conducted? Who will participate? How will they be selected? What information will be collected? What instruments will be used? How will the results be analyzed?

## 3.2.1 Research Locale

### Guidelines

- Describe where the study will be conducted, the nature of the organization, relevant operations, and why the locale was selected.
- Avoid writing a lengthy history of the organization unless it is directly relevant.
### Example

The study will be conducted at ABC Flower Shop in Taguig City. The business provides fresh flowers and floral arrangements and maintains inventory records for its products. The organization was selected because its operations involve inventory monitoring, product spoilage, sales transactions, and stock replenishment, which are directly related to the objectives of the proposed system.

## 3.2.2 Participants/Respondents of the Study

### Guidelines

- Identify the people who will participate and their specific purpose in the research. Clearly distinguish participants used for requirements gathering from participants used for system evaluation when applicable.
### Example

The participants will include the owner, manager, and selected employees who are directly involved in the processes covered by the proposed system. They will provide information regarding the existing workflow, problems encountered, business rules, and system requirements.

Qualified IT experts will also participate in the evaluation of the developed application based on the applicable software quality criteria.

## 3.2.3 Sampling Technique – Purposive Sampling

### Guidelines

- State the sampling technique and explain why it is appropriate, who will be selected, how participants will be selected, the inclusion criteria, and why those criteria are relevant.
- Do not automatically use Slovin's formula for purposive sampling. Sample size should be justified according to the study purpose, qualified participant pool, inclusion criteria, and evaluation requirements.
### Example

The study will employ purposive sampling in selecting participants. This non-probability sampling technique is appropriate because the research requires individuals who possess specific knowledge, responsibilities, experience, or qualifications relevant to the proposed system.

End-user participants will be selected based on their direct involvement in the processes covered by the application. IT experts will be selected based on relevant educational background, professional experience, and expertise in software development, information systems, databases, software testing, or related computing fields.

## 3.2.4 Research Instruments

### Guidelines

- Identify the instruments and explain what each will measure or collect. Possible instruments include semi-structured interview guides, observation checklists, requirements questionnaires, document analysis forms, ISO/IEC 25010-based instruments, and the System Usability Scale (SUS).

### Example

A semi-structured interview guide will be used to gather information concerning the existing process, encountered problems, business rules, user requirements, and expected system functions.

After system development, evaluation instruments will be administered to qualified participants to assess the software quality and usability of the application.

## 3.2.5 Data Gathering Procedure

### Guidelines

- Explain the procedure chronologically. A typical flow is: Permission → Participant Selection → Interview/Observation → Data Collection → Requirements Analysis → System Development → Evaluation → Data Analysis.
### Example

The researchers will first obtain permission from the organization to conduct the study. Participants who satisfy the established inclusion criteria will then be identified through purposive sampling.

Interviews, observations, and document analysis will be conducted to identify existing processes, problems, business rules, and system requirements. Relevant historical records will also be collected with appropriate authorization when required by the study.

After development and testing, qualified participants will evaluate the completed application. Their responses will then be organized, analyzed, and interpreted.

## 3.2.6 System Evaluation Procedure

### Guidelines

- Clearly identify who evaluates what. For example, IT experts may evaluate applicable ISO/IEC 25010 software quality characteristics, while intended end users may complete the System Usability Scale (SUS). Keep system evaluation separate from technical system testing.
### Example

Qualified IT experts will interact with the developed system before evaluating applicable software quality characteristics. Intended users will also perform representative tasks using the system before completing the usability evaluation instrument.

## 3.2.7 Statistical Treatment of Data

### Guidelines

- Identify the statistical procedures and explain where each will be used. Possible techniques include frequency, percentage, mean or weighted mean when appropriate, standard deviation, SUS scoring, and model-specific performance metrics.
### Example

Descriptive statistics will be used to summarize the system evaluation results. Frequency counts and appropriate measures of central tendency will be used to summarize responses from the software quality evaluation instrument. Responses obtained using the System Usability Scale will be calculated according to the prescribed SUS scoring procedure.

## 3.2.8 Ethical Considerations

### Guidelines

- Discuss organizational permission, informed consent, voluntary participation, confidentiality, privacy, data minimization, anonymization, secure data storage, and restricted access.
### Example

Permission will be obtained from the organization before collecting research data. Participants will be informed of the purpose of the study, and their participation will be voluntary. Personally identifiable information that is unnecessary for the research will be excluded or anonymized where appropriate.

Research data will be securely stored and accessed only by authorized researchers. Organizational records will be used only for the purposes stated in the study.
