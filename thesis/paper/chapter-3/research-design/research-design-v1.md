# 3.1 Research Design

The study will employ a descriptive-developmental research design. This design matches the two-part nature of the study: it must first establish the current financial management behaviors, challenges, and needs of the intended users, and then build BUDGIE, a personalized intelligent personal finance management (PFM) application, upon the requirements drawn from that investigation. The descriptive and developmental components are applied sequentially, with the findings of the descriptive phase informing the development and evaluation carried out in the developmental phase.

The descriptive component will be used to examine the existing financial management practices of Filipinos aged 18 to 59 in the National Capital Region, identify the problems they encounter in budgeting, saving, and managing debt, and determine the requirements of the intended users. This addresses the portion of the specific objectives concerned with examining fundamental financial management behaviors and needs; the Public User Expectations and Perception Survey (PUEPS) served as preliminary investigation, and supporting data will be drawn from the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) Consumer Finance Survey and the Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) Family Income and Expenditure Survey. Information will be gathered from participants who are directly involved in the existing financial processes, and will be analyzed using descriptive statistical techniques to establish the baseline conditions that BUDGIE is expected to address.

The developmental component will focus on the design, development, testing, and evaluation of BUDGIE based on the requirements identified during the descriptive phase. The proposed system will provide transaction recording, budgeting, savings goal and debt management, spending forecasts, and anomaly alerts intended to assist Filipino working young adults in managing their finances, with the four model families (classification, forecasting, optimization, and anomaly detection) trained, evaluated, and integrated into the application, and the completed system tested for functionality, performance efficiency, usability, reliability, and security and evaluated using the System Usability Scale and ISO/IEC 25010 metrics. This research design is appropriate because the study does not only examine the existing financial situation but also develops and evaluates a technological solution based on the identified user and system requirements.
