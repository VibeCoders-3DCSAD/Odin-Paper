# Research Design

This study will employ a combination of experimental, developmental, and descriptive research designs to evaluate, develop, and validate the OptiCrew Workforce Management System for FIN-NOYS. The experimental design will be used to compare the performance of two task allocation methods: the hybrid Rule-Based and Genetic Algorithm (RB+GA) and the Traditional Genetic Algorithm (T-GA). Both algorithms will operate under identical task datasets, employee profiles, and scheduling constraints to ensure a fair comparison. The evaluation will focus on three performance metrics, the fitness rate, convergence rate, and run time to determine which algorithm produces more efficient and balanced task scheduling. This phase aims to assess whether the integration of rule-based preprocessing with genetic optimization significantly improves workforce scheduling efficiency and accuracy compared to conventional algorithmic approaches.

The developmental design will guide the creation of the OptiCrew system following the Software Development Life Cycle (SDLC), which includes requirements gathering, system design, development, testing, and deployment. During the requirements phase, both functional and non-functional requirements will be defined, focusing on three primary modules: Employer, Employee, and Client. These modules will include features such as service appointment, task allocation, attendance tracking, scheduling, and performance monitoring. The design phase will produce system architecture diagrams, workflow models, and user interface prototypes that emphasize usability, responsiveness, and security. During development, both the rule-based and genetic algorithm modules will be implemented and integrated to form the hybrid task allocation system. Testing will include unit, integration, and system-level evaluations to verify stability and accuracy before deployment on Hostinger.

The descriptive design will be used to evaluate the system’s overall quality, usability, and user satisfaction through purposive sampling of 60 respondents—30 IT experts and 30 non-IT experts. The IT experts, specializing in software development and evaluation, will assess the system’s functionality, maintainability, and performance, while the non-IT experts, representing the cleaning service industry, will evaluate usability, interaction capability, and effectiveness. Guided by the ISO 25010 software quality model, data will be collected using structured questionnaires and Likert-scale evaluations.
