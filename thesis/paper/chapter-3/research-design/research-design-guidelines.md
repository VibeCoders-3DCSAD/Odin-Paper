# 3.1 Research Design

The Research Design explains the overall approach used to answer the research questions and achieve the objectives of the study.

## Guidelines in Writing

- Identify the selected research design.
- Provide a brief scholarly basis for the design.
- Explain why the design is appropriate for the study.
- Relate the design directly to the research objectives.
- Explain how the design will actually be applied.
- Do not select a research design simply because it was used in a previous thesis.

## Example 1 – Descriptive-Developmental Research Design

Appropriate when the researchers first describe or investigate an existing situation, process, or problem and then develop and evaluate a technological solution.

Example Project: FlowerTrack: Inventory Management System with Flower Spoilage Monitoring

The study will employ a descriptive-developmental research design. The descriptive component will be used to examine the existing inventory practices of the organization, identify problems encountered in monitoring flower stocks and spoilage, and determine the requirements of the intended users. Information will be gathered from participants who are directly involved in the existing inventory processes.

The developmental component will focus on the design, development, testing, and evaluation of FlowerTrack based on the requirements identified during the descriptive phase. The proposed system will provide inventory monitoring and forecasting functions intended to assist the organization in managing its inventory.

This research design is appropriate because the study does not only examine the existing inventory situation but also develops and evaluates a technological solution based on the identified organizational requirements.

## Example 2 – Developmental Research Design

Appropriate when the main purpose is to design, develop, test, and evaluate a software application or technological product.

Example Project: Web-Based Student Appointment and Queue Management System

The study will employ a developmental research design because its primary objective is to design, develop, test, and evaluate a web-based student appointment and queue management system. The development process will begin with the identification and analysis of user and system requirements obtained from intended users and relevant stakeholders.

The identified requirements will serve as the basis for designing and developing the application. The completed system will undergo technical testing and subsequent evaluation to determine whether it satisfies the identified functional, quality, and usability requirements.

## Example 3 – Descriptive Research Design

Appropriate when the primary objective is to describe existing characteristics, perceptions, practices, behaviors, or conditions rather than develop a software solution.

Example Study: Cybersecurity Awareness and Password Security Practices Among College Students

The study will employ a descriptive research design to examine the cybersecurity awareness and password security practices of selected college students. Specifically, the study will describe participants' awareness of cybersecurity threats, password management practices, use of multi-factor authentication, and responses to suspicious online activities.

Data will be collected using an appropriate research instrument and analyzed using descriptive statistical techniques. This design is appropriate because the study seeks to describe existing cybersecurity awareness and practices without manipulating the research environment.

The descriptive research method will be used to assess and interpret user perceptions of the system. Descriptive research focuses on obtaining a clear and accurate understanding of current conditions or opinions by collecting and analyzing data from selected respondents. The system will be evaluated by both IT experts and non-IT experts using standard software evaluation criteria. These include aspects such as Functional Suitability, Interaction Capability, Reliability, Security, and Maintainability. Data will be collected through distributed survey questionnaires or interviews. The responses will be statistically analyzed using tools such as mean and average to interpret the results. The findings will serve as the basis for determining whether the system is acceptable based on inputs from both IT experts and non-IT experts.
